About Robotnik

Robotnik is the live intelligence model of the frontier technology stack. It connects markets, capital, companies, materials, and dependencies across semiconductors, robotics, space, and critical materials to show where the system is changing, where its bottlenecks are, and who is exposed.

One asset class, not four sectors

The four sectors are usually followed separately, by different desks reading different research. Robotnik's premise is that they are one system. The same chips, magnets, and materials run through all of them, so a shock in one surfaces as cost and risk in the others, and the dependencies that matter are the ones the sectors share. Treating the four as a single asset class is what brings those shared dependencies into view. The full argument is set out in the frontier stack thesis.

What Robotnik does

The model places each company in the universe by sector and by value-chain tier, from upstream materials to software. It links each part of the stack to what it depends on, upstream and downstream, and rates the bottlenecks: the points where a critical input is concentrated, often in one country or a single firm. Each link is sourced or marked as curated interpretation, and each rating follows a published method. The Robotnik indices give the headline numbers. The rule that draws the universe, the test for which companies are in and which are out, is documented in the universe methodology.

The method behind each index and each ruling is written down and published in full. The reference library is open, and it is where the workings live.

Who it is for

Investment teams that analyse frontier-technology companies directly: thematic and technology funds, and deep-tech and growth venture funds. Robotnik is built to trace an event, such as an export control or a capacity change, through the stack to the materials, tiers, and companies downstream, and to show how they connect. It maps structural consequences rather than forecasting prices or outcomes.
